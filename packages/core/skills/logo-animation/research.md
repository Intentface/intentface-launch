# Logo animation: sources

The research behind SKILL.md: the terms, how studios and brand teams make logo animations and sonic logos, and the cases they are drawn from (Netflix, Google, Intel, Apple, HBO, Pixar, Mastercard, Airbnb, Slack, Koto, GitHub). Times are given as published (seconds, or frames at 24 to 30 fps); SKILL.md converts them to frames at 60 fps.

## Terms

| Term | What it is | Typical length | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logo sting | A very short animated logo moment, often with a sound hit, opening or closing a video | about 1 to 3 s | Broadcast slang; "sting" also means the audio hit itself |
| Ident | A branded clip that identifies a channel, studio or platform before content | about 3 to 10 s | Variety called Netflix's 2019 animation "an 'ident' in designer lingo" |
| Logo reveal, intro, outro, bumper, end tag | An animated logo at the start or end of a video | intro 3 to 6 s; outro 2 to 5 s | GitHub's toolkit has its Invertocat animation "for end tags and bumpers in marketing videos" |
| Production logo | The film-studio version (Pixar's lamp, 20th Century's fanfare, MGM's lion) | 5 to 20 s | Often a long theatrical cut and a short one; Pixar's short version starts on the finished logo |
| Animated or dynamic logo | A logo whose resting state includes motion | loop or event-driven | Google's dots are "a dynamic and perpetually moving state of the logo" |
| Motion identity, motion language, motion system | The rules for how the whole brand moves | n/a | DixonBaxi's "motion theory": "principles that guide the animation and interaction of any part of the brand experience where motion is needed" |
| Sonic logo, audio logo, audio mnemonic | The short sonic signature | about 1.3 to 3 s (up to 5) | MassiveMusic: "the typical 2-4 second sound"; Made Music's Kevin Perlmutter: "a short, two- to three second expression of the anthem" |
| Sonic identity | The full system: anthem (30 to 60 s), mnemonic, UX sounds, guidelines | n/a | Sixième Son composes "a long track of 40 to 60 seconds that sets the foundation… At its heart lies the sonic logo" |

- PRINT Magazine: "A motion identity is not an animated logo." A motion system "establishes a shared language for movement, providing the logic, behaviours and reason for why a brand moves." "Consistency and time are the two pillars."
- Koto frames it as "Level 1: animated brand assets", then "Level 2: motion systems".

## Discovery

- Airbnb's brand truth was "belonging"; Netflix's brief for its sound was "tension, release, quirky".
- Google's hardest problem was "finding a logo and identity system that worked across such a large spectrum of products, platforms and devices."
- Netflix knew viewers "had no patience for a long intro", so the ta-dum had to be short: frequency of exposure drives length.
- HBO's update kept the "Static Angel" DNA while shortening it for streaming.
- Koto's rollout advice: flag early whether the brand "is going to be able to work with complex motion or 3D assets."

## Motion principles

- **Koto:** "First, you need to create what we call 'motion principles.' These are a set of guiding traits, inspired by your brand strategy… the moment you see a small square move in a way that embodies one of those traits, you'll know you're onto something." Start with "your key brand signifiers: logotype, symbol, and/or headline typography", then grow a system, because "the same assets will become repetitive very quickly!"
- **DixonBaxi** explores "ways in which things can move with intent and purpose", refined into a "motion theory". For Freely, "objects can overshoot or bounce to simulate gravity, giving them weight and life. Subtle flexes of curves help round these motions out with a relaxed softness."
- **GitHub's** published toolkit: motion should Facilitate ("inform and guide"), Engage and Impact ("influential and memorable"). Its style combines "smooth eases contrasting more 'human' stepped animations" for "leveraging AI to empower human ingenuity". Mona Sans Mono is animated "with more of a mechanical rhythm, drawing from the behavior of terminals". Rules: motion "should never feel like an out-of the box presentation effect"; no "slapping, rolling, or bouncing" text unless it is a character's movement; no mascot-style playfulness for "money, security… apologies"; keep it "quick, but legible"; "If you don't have time to get it right, then you should simplify your approach." On context: "Event promos should inspire and delight. Technical demos should be precise. A glitchy effect used during a live-stream may appear broken, not artful."
- **Google (2015):** the dots move "along geometric arcs and following a standard set of snappy easing curves"; "while their movements might seem spontaneous, their motion is rooted in consistent paths and timing."
- International publishes motion primitives as "Easing Curves, Duration and Core Dynamics".

## Process

- Pentagram's Slack exploration spanned "connect-the-dots, complex knots, emojis and people-like shapes."
- Google's Jonathan Jarvis: "Animation was almost the first part of the design process… a ton of decisions are made by keyframes." "Animation is an amazing tool to 'jump to the end.'" On restraint, the second-hardest part: "keeping the motion simple… definitely took discipline. We have a pretty messy cutting room floor." His goal: "fast, simple, clear what was happening and have a charming moment or two."
- Netflix: IndieWire calls the ta-dum "the yearlong effort to create what Netflix terms the 'ta-dum' sound"; Screen Rant reports that Todd Yellin and brand design lead Tanya Kumar chose from "20-30 different sound effects". A focus group, not told what it was for, described the winner as "dramatic," "interesting," "beginning," "movie." The 2019 visual ident took two years.

## Techniques and the marks that invite them

- **Construction:** Slack's octothorpe is "built on a grid" from "a speech bubble and lozenge… that can be extracted and used as graphic elements". The old hashtag (11 colours at a precise 18° rotation) was "extremely easy to get wrong" (Michael Bierut's team, Pentagram, 2019).
- **Line draw:** DesignStudio's James Greenfield on Airbnb's Bélo (2014, "belong anywhere"): "we also wanted to create a symbol that anyone can draw… a great logo is one that you can draw in the sand with your big toe."
- **Morph and states:** Google's dots have expressions for "listening, thinking, replying, incomprehension, and confirmation", become an equalizer while you speak, and spin while searching. The system has three parts: logotype, G, and dots "for interactive, assistive, and transitional moments". Gemini extends the dots into softened, "ethereal" shapes.
- **Container and zoom-through:** Netflix 2019, where the camera flies into the N and its ribbons become spectrum beams.
- **Particles with a reason:** Netflix's beams: "we took the colors from Netflix's vast array of content."
- **Character:** Pixar's lamp squashes the "I"; Koto redrew Animade's infinite-loop logo as "an inquisitive pair of eyes".
- **Motion verbs from the promise:** Koto's Deezer identity is "motion-first", with a heart that "pulsates and reacts in a characterful way to reflect the vibe of each genre"; for Bolt, "motion and sonic branding echo the speed of a click."

## Lockups, the hold, length

- Pixar: the lamp performs on the letters, then "Animation Studios" fades in afterwards (and "a little later" in the closing variant since 2008).
- Netflix's ta-dum was "always meant to be only three or four seconds". HBO's Static Angel runs about 5 s with "a beginning, middle and end." In a theatre the short ta-dum felt "so short and so abrupt", so Hans Zimmer extended it for the theatrical version, which premiered before *Roma* at Venice.
- HBO's 90-second Feature Presentation shows what is too long for how often a logo is seen in streaming.

## Cases

### Netflix: the ta-dum and the ribbon N

- **Sound.** VP of Product Todd Yellin led it. He wanted viewers to think "Wow, I'm going to get a treat", and the sound had to be short and "more cinematic than electronic". He hired Oscar-winning sound editor Lon Bender (*Braveheart*, *Drive*) with the descriptors tension, release, quirky. Candidates included a door opening, strange instruments and filmmaking sounds; for a while the finalist ended with a goat bleat. The final two knocks are Bender's wedding ring on a bedroom cabinet, layered with a slowed anvil and muted thuds. The closing swell (the "blossom") is a reversed-guitar fragment Charlie Campagna recorded in the 1990s through a Digitech GSP2101 and a Lexicon JamMan, used without further processing.
- **Visual (2019).** The first rework in five years: the ribbon N folds up, the camera flies into it, and the ribbons become spectrum beams, "inspired by the spectrum of stories, emotions, languages, fans and creators" and by "turning Netflix's own show thumbnails sideways, like records on a shelf." The background went from white to dark "to better set the mood"; the sound stayed. Made by Imaginary Forces (creative director Michelle Dougherty, producer Keith Bryant, CG supervisor Meng-Yang Lu, lead animator Evan Sexton); the theatrical logo came first. Clio Entertainment 2019 Bronze, "Network/Series Identity Package", for "Netflix Originals Identity 2020".

### Google: the dots (2015)

- A week-long sprint in New York with Creative Lab and Material Design; the brief included "dynamic, intelligent motion that responded to users at all stages of an interaction." Adam Grabowski was a lead motion designer.

### Intel: the bong

- Walter Werzowa composed it in 1994 for "Intel Inside"; the rhythm follows the tagline's syllables. Intel describes "a simple five-note tune… and 20 sounds from those notes." Audiospheric describes the opening as "an audio 'spark' consisting of more than 20 sounds, including a tambourine, an anvil, an electric spark, and a hammer striking a pipe", with synths blended with xylophone and marimba.
- Intel's @IntelTech account: "in a 60-person focus group, only 80% of participants could recognize the tune when played on violin, as opposed to 100% with the correct tone—even with wrong notes added." Werzowa updates the arrangement every 2 to 3 years and keeps the five-note core.

### Apple: the Mac startup chord

- Jim Reekes made it "in my home studio on a Korg Wavestation… a C Major chord, played with both hands stretched out as wide as possible (with 3rd at the top)", as a "palette cleanser" after the old tritone chimes became associated with crashes. Inspired by the last chord of "A Day in the Life", with chorus and reverb; later pitch-shifted to F-sharp major (from the Quadra 840AV). A US registered trademark since 2012.

### HBO: Static Angel

- HBO's originals once opened silently. Bruce Richmond's team built a roughly 5-second "turning on a television" sound: static, a transitional drone, a synthesised choral swell, and a click in the closing variant, which plays the choir reversed. Per Twenty Thousand Hertz (via a Shore Fire Media release), "generating graphics for the five-second clip took three whole days of digital processing." For streaming, HBO worked with Made Music Studio to update the sonic brand without losing the nostalgia.

### Pixar: the Luxo Jr. lamp

- The lamp from the 1986 short hops in, squashes the "I" as it did the ball, and looks at camera; the closing variant dims to dark with a light-switch click. Designing Sound (2011) notes Gary Rydstrom "did his first work for Pixar on the short film Luxo Jr.", and Lasseter credits him with "creating the lamp's voice from the squeak of a lightbulb being screwed in."

### Mastercard: one melody, several lengths

- CMO Raja Rajamannar worked with neurology, psychology and musicology experts on "a 30-second melody that became the DNA", a 3-second signature for ads, and a short acceptance sound at checkout: "1.3-second… made up of six notes" (Marketing Brew, 2022) or "a 1.6-second burst" (Mastercard newsroom, 2024). It plays at "more than 235 million touchpoints" (2022), later "more than 500 million points of interaction" (2024).

## Sonic logos

- **Length:** MassiveMusic 2 to 4 s; Made Music 2 to 3 s; SoundOut "typically 1–3 seconds"; Joel Beckerman: "The best Sonic Logos are quite short — usually no more than two seconds." Made Music's Tostitos logo is "just 1.5 seconds."
- **Shape:** Beckerman: "How do you get a story across in two and a half seconds…? A lot of it was about tension and release… a beginning, a middle, and an end." He suggests pentatonic melodies for cross-cultural appeal. Netflix's call and response is "creating tension and then resolving it really quickly".
- **Organic or electronic:** Netflix avoided sounding "too electronic—like the Xbox sound or the Mac startup chime."
- **Signature sources:** a wedding ring (Netflix), a breath (Audiobrain's Xbox 360 logo), TV static (HBO), a wide synth chord (Mac).
- **Adapting one core:** Sixième Son: "The music adapts, but the DNA stays intact." Antfood's Freevee mnemonic has "a two-part structure" with "a modular swelling element that can flex" while "the core… will always stay the same." amp and Inkbot advise deriving UX sounds from the logo's "DNA". amp notes a shift from "the last three seconds of a commercial" to sonic branding "during the first five seconds."
- **Agency processes** converge on audit and strategy, a sonic mood board, brand-to-sound mapping, several directions for anthem and mnemonic, testing, adaptation and guidelines:
  - MassiveMusic: "collaborative sonic mood board sessions", then MassiveBASS testing ("how well a sound is remembered, how distinctive it is, whether it's appropriate").
  - Sixième Son: quantitative testing of "attribution, recall, brand personality alignment, attention, and likeability".
  - amp: "Brand-to-Sound Mapping" with a "proprietary taxonomy", "48-hour melodic recall tests", and a musicologist's plagiarism review.
  - Made Music: find "where sound and music make the biggest impact"; tested with "SonicPulse® Research".
  - Studio Resonate (SiriusXM), Steve Keller: "Distinctive sonic assets aren't distinctive 'right out of the box'… It's crucial to create guidelines for how and when your brand is heard."

## Loudness for stings

- **EBU R128 s1** (short-form): integrated −23.0 LUFS; "Short-term Loudness Level… shall not exceed −18.0 LUFS"; true peak at most −1 dBTP; "Loudness Range is not useful for short-form content."
- **ATSC A/85** (US): −24 LKFS; short-form content is "measured over their whole program mix". The CALM Act "does not apply to… commercials aired on the internet or via streaming services."
- **Spotify** normalises to −14 LUFS and advises "1 dB headroom for lossy encodings". YouTube's −14 LUFS is widely cited but not documented.
- **Netflix deliverables:** "-27LKFS +/- 2 LU 1770-1 dialog-gated", true-peak limiters "at -2.3 or lower".

## Accessibility

- WCAG 2.3.1 (A): no more than three flashes in any one-second period, unless below the flash thresholds. WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA): motion triggered by interaction can be disabled. WCAG 2.2.2: auto-starting motion longer than five seconds alongside other content needs pause, stop or hide.

## Caveats

- The timing ranges for visual stings, the beat structure and the layer stack in SKILL.md are a synthesis of these cases; no standards body defines them. The sonic-logo lengths come directly from the named agencies and cases.
- Some details rest on secondary or fan sources: Rydstrom's credit on the Pixar logo's sound itself (only fan wikis give it; his Luxo Jr. work is documented) and Intel's notes, which sources give as D♭ D♭ G♭ D♭ A♭ or D♭ G♭ D♭ A♭ B♭. Mastercard's acceptance sound is reported as 1.3 s and as 1.6 s.
- No detailed accounts of the iPhone boot animation were found.
- Not researched from primary sources: 20th Century Fox, McDonald's, Spotify, Dropbox and Stripe, and the agencies ManvsMachine, Collins, Instrument, Wolff Olins, Moving Brands, Sid Lee and Buck. Find their case-study pages before citing them.
