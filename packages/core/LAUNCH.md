# Making launch videos (@intentface-launch/core)

Product launch videos written as code: React scenes rendered frame by frame in headless Chromium and encoded with ffmpeg. Every frame is a pure function of its frame number, so any frame can be rendered alone, in any order, in parallel, identically every time.

## A launch folder

- `launch.config.ts`: marks the folder. `product` is the repo it lives in (its `@/…` paths resolve and its public folder is served, so videos use the product's real components and data); `products` links other repos as `@product/<name>/…`. `secrets` names the env file that holds each secret a capture needs (`{ API_KEY: "../.env.local" }`).
- `videos/<id>/`: `video.tsx` (shots), `brief.md`, `captures/*.capture.ts` + `*.json` manifests, `audio/`, `media/` (images and footage the person brings), `reference/` (their screenshots of what to show; not in frame), `comments.json` (review notes), `licences.json` (where each external file comes from), `out/` (ignored).
- `sfx/`: the launch folder's own sound library, shared by its videos, over core's starter pack (`pnpm launch sfx`).
- Commands run from the product's root as `pnpm launch <command>` (or `intentface-launch <command>` anywhere inside it).

## Commands

```sh
pnpm launch dev                            # studio at http://localhost:4400
pnpm launch new <id> --title "…"           # scaffold a video
pnpm launch look <url> --dark --do "click:Run all" --wait 8 --do "click:Add column"  # pictures + fonts, colours, radii
pnpm launch capture <id> [name]            # record a live app, frame-stepped; prints marks that moved
pnpm launch marks <id> <cap> <snap> --select "li"  # marks from a saved snapshot, no recapture
pnpm launch still <id> --at hook:+30,ask:end --grid  # frames to look at, as one labelled image
pnpm launch sheet <id> --shot fly --every 4  # contact sheet of a shot's motion
pnpm launch sheet <id> --beats             # one tile per beat: does something happen on each?
pnpm launch still <id> --at hero --samples 8  # 3D stages as a quick draft (sheet too); --stage-debug shows their passes
pnpm launch measure <id> --shot hero        # motion as numbers: the frame it lands, the hold after, judder in px
pnpm launch diff <id> --frame cut:end --against next  # side by side + difference, and how much differs where
pnpm launch board <id>                     # storyboard + animatic → out/board/, and text shortfalls per shot
pnpm launch render <id> --preview          # review cut: 960×540 30 fps, 3D at 8 samples, no motion blur; minutes, not a final
pnpm launch render <id>                    # 1080p60 final
pnpm launch render <id> --resume           # after a failed or stopped render: only the missing chunks
pnpm launch render <id> --shots app,code   # after changing a shot: draw only those again, reuse the rest of the last render
pnpm launch render <id> --check            # render, then check
pnpm launch watch                          # in a terminal: a live bar for the render running (one Claude started)
pnpm launch render <id> --gif --poster end  # also a GIF under 8 MB and a poster (PNG + 1200×630 JPEG)
pnpm launch render <id> --alpha             # also ProRes 4444 with alpha, keyed as the video's `alpha` says
pnpm launch beats <id>                     # beats.json from audio/
pnpm launch beats <id> --seams             # also the bars that can replay to lengthen it (audio.edit)
pnpm launch sound <id> --png               # the audio as heard as a timeline: impacts, booms, cuts, risers, pulses, tail, in seconds and frames
pnpm launch mix <id>                       # music + sfx + voice, mastered → out/mix.wav (render does this itself)
pnpm launch sfx find whoosh --max 1        # the sound library: core's starter pack plus the launch folder's sfx/
pnpm launch sfx index                      # measure new sounds in sfx/ into sfx/index.json
pnpm launch captions <id>                  # out/<id>.srt and .vtt (render writes them too)
pnpm launch licences <id>                  # every external file and its licence → out/licences.json
pnpm launch render <id> --format vertical  # another shape the video declares (also still, sheet, check)
pnpm launch render <id> --variant all      # the video and each of its variants (also check; one variant: any command)
pnpm launch levels <id> [file]             # per-frame loudness, bands (sub to high), onsets, phrases → <file>.levels.json
pnpm launch check [id]                     # source checks; with an id, checks the rendered video too
pnpm launch addons | add <name> | update   # add-ons, and refreshing skills and guides
```

## The workflow

A launch video shows the product working: its real UI, close up, alive, one step of a task leading to the next, in the product's own look. Text is a few large words naming what was just shown, never a slideshow of feature names. The product-launch-video skill is the method; follow it from the first step.

1. **Discover.** Read the product's code and docs, run it, and look at it (`pnpm launch look <url> --do "click:…"` pictures each step in one image and prints the page's fonts, colours, radii and tokens, no script needed): its type, colours and shapes are the video's look (a brand add-on applies only when the person asks for that brand). Then ask the person what only they know: what sets it apart, the moment a new user says "oh", the before, the audience, the limits. Write `videos/<id>/brief.md`: the claim, one hero feature and two or three supports, each with the moment it can be seen, every fact with its source. Never put a claim on screen that the brief or the product does not support.
2. **Script.** One person doing one real task, each feature the next thing that happens in it, as a table of picture, words and hand-off. The person agrees it before any shot is built.
3. **Music.** Intentface Launch does not make music; it cuts the video to a track, so get the track before the sketch and build around it. The person's own track (bought, licensed) goes straight in `audio/`. Otherwise write the brief from the script (music-brief skill): exact length, BPM (a whole number of frames per beat: 90, 100, 120, 144 at 60 fps), sections with timestamps, and the hits (cuts, logo). The person generates it (Suno, ElevenLabs Music and the like) and drops it in `audio/`. Run `pnpm launch beats <id>`: `beats.json` holds the beats, downbeats, onsets, sections (where the track changes, and whether energy rises), breaths (quiet stretches) and a confidence. If the detected BPM drifts, cut on `beatGrid(beats, fps)` instead of `tempo()`. To lengthen the track by whole bars, `beats <id> --seams` lists the passages that replay cleanly (a score per seam, and a crossfade), and `audio: { edit: [{ replay: [16, 24], times: 1 }] }` plays beats 16 to 24 once more after beat 24, in `render`, `mix`, `check` and the studio; pass the edit to `beatGrid(beats, fps, offset, edit)` and the grid follows. Then `pnpm launch sound <id> --png` reads the track the way a sound designer hears it: the events (impact, boom, cut to boom, riser, pulse, tail) with seconds and frames, loudness with its short-term maximum, and a spectrogram with a frame ruler; time the hits to those frames, not to `beats` onsets, which miss a band-limited boom. With no music for now, pick a BPM and time in beats; a track can be briefed to the locked cut later.
4. **Sketch.** One capture run with a `snapshot` and marks at every state (`captures/<name>.capture.ts` against the locally running product, in its demo or seeded mode, dev overlays hidden; commit the `.json` manifest, not the frames). Then every shot of `video.tsx` flat and cheap: the real UI, camera and cursor on marks, the final copy, timing on the track's beats, `says`, `copy`, `camera`, `sound`, `notes`; no 3D, no motion blur. Run `pnpm launch board <id>`: it also runs check's text pass and prints, per shot, text held too briefly to read, too small or outside the safe area (the studio's Board view lists them under the stills), so fix those holds before the person signs off the timing. Reviewers open the Board view in the studio (`/?v=<id>&board`), watch the animatic and leave notes per shot, saved to `videos/<id>/comments.json`. Read that file, act on open notes, and answer by setting `reply` (and `resolved` when done). `--html` also writes a standalone page for reviewers outside the repo. Nothing heavy is built until the person agrees the sketch.
5. **Build.** Replace sketch shots with finished ones, one at a time, keeping their agreed timing, framing and copy: frame captures for the product's own motion, UI state changes, 3D and motion blur where planned. Sound effects (clicks, ticks, whooshes, keystrokes) are `sfx` on the shots, on their frames: `render` mixes them into the music and masters the result to the loudness target, and the studio plays the same mix. Start from the library (`pnpm launch sfx find`, then `src: "sfx:whoosh"`), and generate only what it lacks. A voice-over is `audio.voice` (the music ducks under it), stems are `audio.stems` (mute one over a breath). The sound-design skill has the workflow, including generating with ElevenLabs. After each, look at stills and a contact sheet yourself (the Read tool shows PNGs). Do not report a shot as done without looking at it.
6. **Check, then render.** `pnpm launch check <id>` and resolve every error (see below). Then `pnpm launch render <id>` for the mp4, `--master` for ProRes, `--alpha` for ProRes 4444 with transparency, `--scale 2` for 4K.

## Rules for scene code

- **Time comes only from `useFrame()`.** No `Date.now()`, `performance.now()`, `requestAnimationFrame`, `setTimeout`, CSS transitions or `Math.random()` in scenes. Use `interpolate`, `progress`, `spring`, `random(seed)`. Keyframed CSS animations are fine: the engine pauses them and sets their time from the enclosing shot.
- **One scene across a cut.** When two shots show one continuous scene (a 3D stage, a drifting backdrop) and the cut should be invisible, give the later shot `continues: "ask"`: its `useFrame()` counts from the start of shot "ask", so a component both shots render carries on (camera, lights, handheld sway, drift) instead of restarting. `useFrame({ from: "ask" })` reads that clock for one value anywhere. Moments, `sfx` and `still --shot` frames still count from the shot's own start.
- **Frames can be fractional.** Motion-blur samples render at `frame + 0.25` and so on. Everything must accept non-integer frames.
- **Anything async registers with `waitFor(promise)`** (image decode, a canvas that loads data) so the renderer does not capture a frame too early. `Capture` already does.
- **Canvas and WebGL paint in `useLayoutEffect`** from the current frame, synchronously. Text they draw is invisible to `check` unless declared: `declareText(canvas, [{ text, x, y, width, height, size }])` in the same effect, every frame, in the canvas's CSS pixels (an empty list when it shows none; `bleed: true` for type larger than the frame on purpose, which skips the safe margin); leave the text out while `isTextHidden()` is true for exact contrast. A layer whose own box cuts what it draws (a globe taller than its canvas) is flagged by `check`; `data-launch-crop="ok"` when that is meant.
- **Captured UI lives in content space.** A `Capture` or `Snapshot` is laid out at the capture's CSS size inside a `Camera`; marks from the manifest (`mark(capture, "results:pill-ai")`) are in the same pixels, so the camera can frame any element. Give the `Camera` the capture and name marks in its keys (`view: "editor:instructions+editor:options"` frames the union of both), so framings follow the UI when a recapture moves it; numbers do not.
- **Close-ups use snapshots, not frames.** `c.snapshot(name, { marks })` in a capture script saves the page's DOM, CSS, fonts and images; `<Snapshot>` rebuilds it in an iframe, sharp at any zoom, and its `animate(api, frame)` moves single elements (rows, pills) or changes text for one frame at a time. `api.isolate(el)` draws only one element on a transparent page and `api.hide(el)` leaves one out, to split a UI into layers. `api.type(input, text, { blinkFrom })` shows a partly typed value with a caret in the product's own field, and `api.textRects(el, text, { by: "word" | "char" })` gives the boxes of its words in the snapshot's own font and layout (for a field, as the value would lay out), in content pixels. Measure in `onLoad={(api) => setState(…)}` to place overlays or Camera keys outside the snapshot: it runs before the frame is let go. Never measure snapshot text with a canvas. Raster frames are for the product's own motion (map flights, loading states). A `<canvas>` (a data grid, a chart, a map) stays a picture in a snapshot: `c.snapshot(name, { canvasScale: 4 })` redraws the page at 4 device pixels per CSS pixel for that picture, sharp through a 2× push-in. When single cells must change, save the grid's rows with `c.data` and rebuild it as DOM in the product's look (`useProductLook`).
- **Text beside the UI uses the product's faces.** `const look = useProductLook(capture)` returns `fonts.body`, `fonts.heading`, `fonts.mono` (CSS stacks), `colors.background`, `surface`, `text`, `muted`, `accent`, `border` and `radii`, recorded with each snapshot, and registers the snapshot's `@font-face` rules on the video's page (the frame waits for them), so a headline is never set in a fallback face.
- **Name framings by marks.** A Camera key's `view` takes mark names (`"editor:instructions+editor:options"`), which follow the UI through a recapture; numbers do not. A recapture prints every mark that moved more than 16 px since the last manifest: check the shots that frame them. A rect or mark `view` is fitted with `padding` (0.12 of the frame by default); when the rect is already the exact framing (a whole 1920×1080 app that the next shot starts from), pass `padding: 0`, or the cut jumps.
- **Hold text long enough to read, and no longer.** (The kinetic-typography skill has the rest of how type moves.) `readingTimeOnBeats(text, t)` gives the hold after the text lands, in whole beats (`t` from `tempo()`); a brand may set stricter options. It is a floor: a line of a few words gets about a bar, then the film moves on. Keep headlines to 6 words and lines to 42 characters (`lintText`). Held text holds still: a slow push or drift on DOM text steps a pixel at a time (glyphs snap to whole pixels), so move the picture behind it instead, or, where the camera should keep pushing in on the text (an end card), draw it as a picture with `TextImage`, which scales smoothly, and let spring-driven text land on whole pixels: `landed(x, target)` keeps the move fractional and snaps to the rounded target once within 0.2 px (rounding every frame makes the slow tail step a pixel at a time). Type that scales does so about its baseline: about its centre, the baseline lands between pixels and the line climbs the pixel grid a pixel at a time (0.29 px of judder against 0.04 px, measured); `Slam` and `Reveal` do it, `useBaseline()` for your own.
- **Motion design, not slideshow.** The product's UI carries the story; text names what it just showed. Type lands on beats (`Slam`), the camera never sits still for long, and shots hand off with continuity (match cuts on size and position, the same object carried from shot to shot). One idea per shot.
- **Keep motion moving.** Fluid motion is mostly continuity of speed: a thing that is moving stops only where it lands.
  - A value that changes target more than once (a UI state, a size, a position) uses `springTo(frame, fps, [{ at, to }, …], spring)`: one spring per change, summed, so a change mid-flight keeps the speed it had. Never chain `progress()` calls that each start from rest.
  - Camera and Cursor keys take `travel`: longer than the gap to the previous key, the next move sets off before the last one lands, and the camera or cursor rounds the corner instead of stopping on a waypoint. Keep full stops for clicks and holds. A Camera key with `cut: true` jumps to its view on its frame instead: a cut in closer (or back out) inside the shot while the action carries on.
  - Springs by feel: `{ duration: t.beats(1), bounce: 0.1 }` in place of stiffness and damping. `springEase(bounce)` is a spring as an easing that still arrives when the key says (for Camera and Cursor keys, `interpolate`, `progress`).
  - A point moves as one: `const [x, y] = springTo(frame, fps, [{ at, to: [x0, y0] }, …], spring)`; a colour with `springColor(frame, fps, [{ at, to: "#4f46e5" }, …], spring)`, in OKLCH (hue the short way, low bounce).
  - Speed carries across a cut: `springToVelocity(frame, …)` at the last frame of a shot is the `velocity` of the next shot's first target, so a move that leaves one shot arrives in the next still moving. `settleFrames(spring, fps)` is how long a move takes to come to rest, for placing the next beat after it.
- **One shape, many states.** When the story is one piece of UI becoming the next (button to loader to check, a toggle opening into tabs), keep it one element whose size, radius and colour ride springs, and do not cut. Its content changes with `<Swap>`: the old content leaves fast, the new arrives a few frames later, once the container has room. Content always trails its container by 2 to 4 frames; a container and its content moving in lockstep looks pasted on.
- **UI physics.** A selection that travels (tab indicator, segmented control, toggle knob) uses `edges()`: the leading edge rides a stiffer spring than the trailing one, so it stretches toward where it is going. A drag is direct manipulation: `drag: true` on a Cursor key holds the button, `dragValue()` computes the value from the pointer while held and springs from where it was let go (at the speed it was moving) on release, and `rubberband()` makes a limit give instead of stopping hard.
- **No `will-change`.** Chrome rasterises a `will-change: transform` layer once and scales the bitmap, so text under a Camera zoom goes soft. Frames are seeked, not animated, so it buys nothing; `check` flags it.
- **Picture that lands on a sound** declares it: `hits: [{ at: 0, sound: "boom" }]` on the shot (`at` a frame from the shot's start or a moment name; `sound` seconds into the audio as heard, or an event `sound` names: `boom`, `impact`, `cut`, `onset`, `riser` for its end). `check` finds the nearest such event and fails when the two are more than 2 frames apart (`tolerance`).
- **Moving with the sound** (a voice orb, a meter, a logo that breathes with the music) reads the audio analysed ahead of time, never live: `pnpm launch levels <id> <file>` writes envelopes per frame (`rms`, `sub`, `low`, `mid`, `high`, 0 to 1 against the track's loud level), `onsets` and `phrases` (sound between silences). Import the JSON in the scene and read it with `audioLevels(data)`: `at(t, band)`, `smooth(t, window)`, `pulse(t)` on onsets, `accumulate(t)` as a phase that runs faster while it is loud, `phraseAt(t)`. A voice keeps the level high, so drive size from each syllable's rise above the slow average (`at - smooth`), not the level alone. Cues from narration can come from `phrases` and the script, with words placed by syllables inside a phrase; no forced alignment needed.
- **Transitions** (`transitions.*`, on the incoming shot) take their length in frames and an `easing`: `fade`, `blur`, `zoom`, `push`, `wipe` (any angle, feathered), `iris` (a circle from a point), `shape` (any SVG path growing from a point), `clock`, `zoomInto` (push into a rect of the outgoing shot that the next one opens out of), `whip`, `sweep` (a band of light; the cut happens under it), `displace`, `luma`, `chromatic`, `honest` (a short dissolve that admits the edit skipped time), and `pageCurl` (the outgoing shot peels off like a sheet of paper from an edge or a corner, its shadow on the next shot; `paper: "vellum"` and the other translucent papers show the next shot through the lifting sheet). A page curl draws the outgoing shot once per strip of the curl (and with a translucent paper, the next one too): keep them to DOM, images and stills. A WebGL layer in one (a `LiquidMetal`, a `Shaders` layer) runs the page out of contexts; from the frame the curl starts, draw it as a still (`LiquidMetal`'s `still`: one picture every strip shares), and draw large canvases as pictures. A see-through sheet (a `PageFlip` leaf or a curl) costs a copy of what is under it in every strip, and close up (3× zoom) strips went missing from renders for a frame at a time, in sequence only (stills looked fine): keep its paper free of `texture` (a multiplied layer in every strip) and its `blur` light (1 to 2 px), and give a close-up's book only the pages its frame sees. A curl that need not show the next shot through it can be nearly opaque (`seeThrough` under 0.1 copies nothing). Keep a `PageFlip`'s e-ink `refresh` to one landing or none (`refresh={0}`): repeated redraws read as flicker; a small book (under about 0.7×) wants `thickness={0}`. `blur`, `zoom`, `whip`, `displace`, `luma` and `chromatic` filter the shots: never over a `Stage3D`. A match cut carries an element across: `transitions.matchCut({ from, to, velocity })` starts the incoming shot with the element where (and as fast as) it was in the last shot and springs it home; `frames: 0` keeps the cut hard, and `cut.at(frame)` on just the element's layer keeps the background still.
- **Other shapes reflow, they are not cropped.** `formats: { vertical: { width: 1080, height: 1920 }, square: { … } }` on the video adds shapes beside its own; scenes read `useVideoInfo().format` (`name`, `width`, `height`, `aspect`) and lay out for it, and `clearArea(format)` is the rect clear of the safe margin and, in 9:16, the apps' UI (in 1080×1920 about x 96 to 960, y 380 to 1248). Lay a vertical cut out in `safeZone(format)` (x 90 to 880, y 270 to 1470 for organic posts; `"ads"` is stricter) and let backgrounds fill the frame; the social-formats skill says which shapes to deliver for which platform. `--format <name>` on `render`, `still`, `sheet` and `check` picks one; outputs get `-<name>`. Shots and timing are shared by every shape.
- **Variants of one video** (an episode's last line, a language) are one folder: `variants: { ep02: { audio: { voice }, data: { line: "…", levels }, shots: { outro: { duration: 300, copy: ["…"] } } } }` on the video. A variant merges its `audio` over the video's, replaces `sfx` and `captions` when given, and changes shots by id (length, copy, direction, moments, sfx). Scenes read `useVariant()` (`name`, `data`) or `useVideoInfo().variant`. `--variant ep02` works on every command; `render` and `check` also take `--variant all`. Outputs get `-<name>` (`<id>-ep02.mp4`, `check-ep02.json`), beside the video's own.
- **Transparent video** (a sting or a title to lay over someone else's picture in an editor): `render --alpha` also writes `<id>-alpha.mov`, ProRes 4444 with alpha. `alpha` on the video says what is transparent. `"page"` (the default) leaves the background out, so type and UI keep their own opacity. `alpha: "black"` with `background: "#000000"` keys light on black by brightness (After Effects' Unmult): each pixel's alpha is its brightest channel, so glows, bloom and `Stage3D` light (which have no opacity of their own) lay over footage as light. Under `"black"`, anything that lifts black keys into a veil: give stages `grain={0}`, and keep dark shapes out (black reads as hole, not as shadow). For a transition between two clips (a black that builds in over one and breaks up onto the next), the black has to be opaque, so use `"page"` with your own background layer that builds in and out, and keep a `Stage3D` (opaque, since its light has none of its own) to the frames where that background is whole. `still --alpha --grid` shows frames on a checkerboard. Check a start or end meant to be fully transparent in the .mov itself (`ffmpeg … alphaextract`).
- **Captions** are data: `captions: [{ text, at, until?, shot? }]` on the video. A cue without `until` holds for its reading time; text is wrapped at 42 characters, two lines a cue, longer text split over several cues. `render` writes `.srt` and `.vtt` beside the mp4; `burnCaptions: true` (or a style) also draws them in the frame, above the platform UI in 9:16.
- **Motion blur is switched on per layer**, as in After Effects: wrap what moves fast in `<MotionBlur when={[[from, to]]}>`, limited to the frames where it moves. On those frames the renderer captures `samples` subframes and averages them in 32-bit, so everything in frame blurs by its own motion and dark gradients do not band. The studio previews the blur in the DOM with `plus-lighter` copies (approximate, can band); stills, sheets, boards and renders use the exact path. `render --blur N` forces supersampling on every changing frame.

## Reviewing your own work

Nothing goes to the person (a board, a still, a render) until you have read stills of every shot and fixed what you found: awkward perspective (a tilted plane's edge in frame), bad crops (UI cut at a frame or tile edge, a shape cropped instead of reflowed), UI floating on a background it was not designed for, muddy fades, the same copy twice on screen, an off-balance composition, jargon, effects that show nothing, and pacing that drags. The motion-review skill has the full list. Every board should look like the finished film's motion design (springs, UI choreography, motion blur on fast moves), not a flat slideshow; the person should never have to ask for quality.

- Stills: `pnpm launch still <id> --at <shot>,<shot>:+30,<shot>:end --grid` for composition, type and spacing, one image for all of them. Name frames by shot, never by absolute number: `shot:end` is its last frame before the outgoing transition, `shot:click+6` a frame the shot names in `moments: { click: 140 }`, and both survive a retime. Each still is printed with its shot and any transition it is in. `--crop x,y,w,h` magnifies a detail (an edge, a glyph, a handoff) without reading a whole 1080p frame. On 3D shots, `--samples 8` draws every stage as a draft for composition (stills and sheets); look at the full samples before calling a shot done.
- Motion: `pnpm launch sheet <id> --shot <shot> --every 3`. Uneven steps between neighbouring tiles mean bad easing; a jump means a missing frame or a readiness bug; tiles bunching up in the middle of a path mean the motion stops at a waypoint (overlap the moves with `travel`, or use `springTo`).
- Handoffs: `pnpm launch diff <id> --frame <shot>:end --against <next>` renders both frames, puts them side by side with a difference picture (changed pixels in magenta) and prints how much differs and where ("0.09% of pixels over 16 levels (max 227), in 860,440 202×200"); `--crop` on that box magnifies it. `--against` also takes an image file (an approved logo PNG, compared at its own resolution) or, with `--against-video v`, another video's frame.
- Motion as numbers: `pnpm launch measure <id> --shot <shot>` (`--crop` one element) renders every frame and prints the frame the motion visibly lands, how long it then holds, and judder: how far the ink strays from a smooth path. Over 0.1 px on motion slower than a pixel a frame shows as shaking. An ease-out lands visibly well before its duration (a 16-frame `ease.expo` at about frame 11; the rest moves under half a pixel a frame), so time the next beat from where it lands, not from the duration.
- Rhythm: `pnpm launch sheet <id> --beats` shows one tile per beat (`--offset 6` to see where each beat's motion has got to). A run of identical tiles is dead time.
- Watch for: text under 28 px, a UI shown so far away its own text is under 20 px (a whole 1440 px app on a 1920 frame sets 14 px body text at about 19: an establishing second at most, then go in), text outside the 96 px safe area, a camera push beyond the capture's scale (soft text), cursor landing off its target, stale capture frames, two camera moves in one shot.
- The whole video: `pnpm launch check <id>` samples the render every 0.1 s and writes `out/check.json`. It measures every piece of text on screen as the viewer sees it (after camera zoom, opacity and clipping) for reading time, size, safe area, clearance (overlay text closer than half its size to a panel, card, window or Snapshot beside it, or touching one, is an error; text sitting on a surface is not beside it, and `data-launch-clearance="ok"` marks a surface or line meant to touch) and contrast against the actual pixels behind it (4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text: 32 px, or 24 px bold, at 1080p). It also flags stretches over 2 s where nothing moves, cuts off the beat (from `beats.json` or `bpm`), storyboard `copy` that never appears, external files with no licence recorded (warnings: a `<file>.license.json` beside the file, or `licences.json` in the video's folder), in 9:16 text under the apps' own UI (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), caption cues shorter than their reading time, layers that cut their own content (a straight edge where a canvas, an SVG or a bare clipping box slices what it draws, as warnings), held frames that differ from their reference (`matches`, below), declared `hits` more than 2 frames off their sound (found in the music and voice without the sound effects, so an effect placed on a hit does not move it), DOM text that creeps (moves or resizes by less than a pixel a frame for half a second: it steps a pixel at a time; a canvas that declares its text scales smoothly and is not flagged), and loudness (-14 LUFS, -1 dBTP; per video, `audio.loudness` sets another target, a delivery spec by name: `"web"` (the default), `"podcast"` (-16), `"broadcast"` (EBU R128, -23), `"sting"` (R128 s1: -23 with the loudest 3 s at most -18) or `"atsc"` (-24, -2 dBTP), which `mix` masters to as well, or `"as-delivered"` for audio that must not be processed, such as an approved narration). The summary counts problems per check first: many errors of one check are usually one decision (a reading rule, a margin), not many fixes. Errors must be fixed; warnings need a decision.
- A frame that must be exact gets `matches` on its shot: `matches: { at: "end", reference: () => <Lockup />, label: "lockup" }`. `check` renders the frame, then the reference alone on the video's background at the same frame, and fails when more than 0.1% of pixels differ by over 16 levels (`tolerance` on the match, or `checks.match` from a brand or launch.config.ts), writing the three pictures to `out/check/`. Use it for the end card against the approved static logo (a brand add-on's lockup component), and for any handoff that must land pixel for pixel.
- Text that is not meant to be read (a montage of cards flying past, product UI built from real components) gets `data-launch-check="off"` on its container; decoration that is not the product's UI either (a field of tiles behind a headline) gets `"decor"`. Text in `Snapshot`s and 3D panels is never checked for reading. UI text is still measured for one thing: `check` warns (`ui`) when, for a second or more, most of the UI's text (in `Snapshot`s and marked `off`) is under 20 px on screen at 1080p (`checks.uiText`): the UI is shown too far away to read. so `check` draws 3D stages at 8 samples (`--samples N` to change). A cut that is off the beat on purpose gets `offBeat: true` on its shot. Thresholds come from core, then installed add-ons, then `checks` in `launch.config.ts`, then the video's own `checks.reading` in `defineVideo` (a video outside a brand installed in the repo keeps core's reading times there).

## Performance (M-series Mac, 12 cores)

- Stills and sheets: seconds. Preview render: ~13 s per 10 s of flat video, and 3D draws at 8 samples there. A final render of a film with heavy 3D and motion blur takes 10 minutes or more (one 63 s film: 12), so render the final once, after the person has signed off a preview cut, and after a change to one shot redraw only that shot (`render <id> --shots <id>`); the chunks of the last render are kept for it.
- Final render: ~46 captured frames per second across 8 workers. Workers are pages in one browser (on Metal they draw as fast as a browser each, at half the memory), each with its own encoder, and by default `render` starts as many as the memory free fits, about 0.35 GB each, up to 8: workers that push the machine into swap make the render no faster and the desktop stall (8 on a 16 GB Mac already in swap: 2 GB more swapped, no faster than 6). It says so when it starts fewer; `--workers N` chooses. Renders and checks run at a lower CPU priority, so the desktop and the person's own browser come first. A frame inside a `MotionBlur` range costs `samples` captures (16 by default), so keep `when` ranges tight. A 21 s video with 78 blurred frames renders in about 2 minutes.
- A frame that takes over 90 s to seek or screenshot is reported with the step it stalled in and the `waitFor` labels still pending (pass a label: `waitFor(promise, "Globe tiles")`). `render` retries it in a new browser, up to twice. A blur or zoom transition over a 3D stage stalls this way on every attempt (Chromium's compositor never delivers the screenshot under several workers): cut to and from 3D shots, or fade with opacity only.
- `render` works in 1 s chunks that workers take from a queue. A chunk that still fails is reported and the rest carry on; `render <id> --resume` (same flags) keeps every finished chunk and renders only what is missing, also after a stopped or crashed run. On `Stage3D` shots the GPU is the limit, not the workers: about 0.65 frames per second in total at 96 samples, whether 4 or 8 workers share it (a 12 s stretch of 3D is a 20 minute render; one worker drew `test-devices` at 3.7 frames per second, four at 3.6), and more workers only stall more; use `--workers 4`. In a log, `render` prints progress at least every 15 s: a slow stretch shows counts rising, a stuck one shows the same count. Your output is a log, so when you start a long render, tell the person they can follow it live in a terminal with `pnpm launch watch` (it draws the bar, the time left and the final encode, prints each render's result, and waits for the next).
- The renderer's Chromium uses the GPU (ANGLE on Metal on macOS). Capture: ~2 frames per second for WebGL-heavy apps at 2× (measured before the GPU switch; likely faster now). Plan captures, do not iterate on them.
- First run on a machine: `pnpm launch setup` downloads the browser; ffmpeg must be installed (`brew install ffmpeg`), and uv for beat detection.
