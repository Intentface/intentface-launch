## A launch folder

A product repo keeps its videos in `launch/`. `launch/CLAUDE.md` tells Claude
how to work there, including anything your team writes down about how it makes
videos. Each video is a folder:

| Path | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `launch.config.ts` | Marks the folder and points at the product, so videos can import its components. |
| `videos/<id>/video.tsx` | The video: its shots, timing and copy. |
| `videos/<id>/brief.md` | The claim, the features and where each one can be seen. |
| `videos/<id>/captures/` | Scripts that record the running product, and their manifests. |
| `videos/<id>/audio/` | The music track and its beats, sound effects, a voice-over. |
| `videos/<id>/media/` | Images and clips you bring for the video: a logo sting, a photo, a screen recording. |
| `videos/<id>/reference/` | Your screenshots of what to show. Claude reads them; they do not appear in the video. |
| `videos/<id>/licences.json` | Where each external file comes from, and its licence. |
| `videos/<id>/comments.json` | Review notes left on the storyboard. |
| `videos/<id>/out/` | Stills, boards and renders. Not committed. |

## The workflow

Each step is agreed with you before the next one starts. What you hand over
at the start (a sentence, screenshots, a track, a recipe your team wrote down)
decides how much Claude asks; the steps stay the same. [Recipes](/recipes)
has ways to start.

1. **Discover.** Claude reads the product and runs it, then asks what only you
   know: what sets it apart and who it is for. The result is `brief.md`.
2. **Script.** One person doing one real task, each feature the next thing
   that happens in it.
3. **Music.** Intentface Launch does not make music, but it cuts the video to
   a track, so the track comes before the sketch. Use music you already have,
   or generate one (Suno, ElevenLabs Music and the like) from the brief Claude
   writes from the script. Drop it in `audio/`, and `pnpm launch beats` finds
   the beats, sections and quiet breaths that the shots are timed to.
4. **Sketch.** Claude captures the product and builds every shot flat and
   cheap, on the track's beats. `pnpm launch board` makes a storyboard and an
   animatic, and lists under each shot any text held too briefly to read; you
   leave notes per shot in the studio.
5. **Build.** Shots are finished one at a time: the product's own motion, UI
   state changes, 3D and motion blur where planned. Claude checks each one in
   stills and contact sheets.
6. **Check and render.** `pnpm launch check` measures every piece of text on
   screen for reading time, size, contrast and room from the UI beside it, and
   flags layers that cut their own content and end frames that differ from the
   approved logo. Then `pnpm launch render` makes the mp4.

## Underneath

- **Frames, not playback.** Time comes only from `useFrame()`. The renderer
  seeks each frame and screenshots it, so nothing depends on how fast a
  machine is.
- **Captures.** A capture script drives the running product in a browser one
  exact frame at a time. A snapshot saves the page's DOM and CSS, so close-ups
  stay sharp at any zoom.
- **Rendering.** Work is split into one-second chunks that workers take from a
  queue. Workers are pages in one headless browser, as many as the free memory
  fits, at a lower CPU priority so the desktop stays responsive. Frames with
  motion blur render several subframes and average them. A stopped render
  resumes with `--resume`.
- **Skills.** The method lives in skills that Claude loads: product-launch-video,
  creative-direction, kinetic-typography, ui-motion, micro-animations,
  logo-animation, motion-review, music-brief, sound-design and social-formats.
