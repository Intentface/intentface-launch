title: Compared with other tools
description: Other ways to make videos with code and agents, what each is good for, and where Intentface Launch fits among them.

Intentface Launch is an exploration. We built it for our own launch videos and
keep developing it as we explore programmatic video. It is made to be shaped
by product teams around their own product, brand and way of working, more a
harness to adapt than a finished tool. It is MIT, and we do not sell it, host
it or have a paid tier, so we have no stake in which tool you pick. This page
is a map of good options, each described on its own terms. Most are free to
try: make the same short video in two or three of them and see what works
best for you.

Checked on 18 October 2026 against each project's own README, docs and
package registry. These projects move fast; if something here is out of date,
please open an issue.

## Where to start

A starting point by what you are making, not a ranking. Several of these
combine well, and many teams will use more than one.

- **Videos from a website URL, a script or a brief, including explainers,
  captions, talking-head recuts and decks, across many coding agents, with
  cloud rendering:** [HyperFrames](#hyperframes).
- **A general React framework for video, for production work, with an
  embeddable player and serverless rendering:** [Remotion](#remotion).
- **Polished product-demo shots on Remotion (chat streams, terminals, device
  frames), themed like shadcn/ui:** [snapcn](#snapcn).
- **Diagrams and technical explainers drawn on a canvas:**
  [Motion Canvas or Revideo](#motion-canvas-and-revideo).
- **Maths and algorithms, in Python:** [Manim](#manim).
- **A launch video built from your product's own source and running UI,
  adapted to your team's brand and workflow, with Claude Code on a Mac:**
  [Intentface Launch](#intentface-launch).

## At a glance

| | Intentface Launch | HyperFrames | Remotion | snapcn | Motion Canvas, Revideo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **You write** | React shots | HTML, CSS, GSAP and the like | React | Remotion components | TypeScript scenes |
| **Licence** | MIT | Apache-2.0 | Free for individuals and small teams; a company licence above 3 employees | MIT, plus paid Pro | MIT |
| **Agents** | Claude Code | Most coding agents | Official skills | Skills, MCP with Pro | None official |
| **Your UI** | Imported and captured | Captured from a URL | Built or imported | Recreated | Drawn |
| **Cloud rendering** | No | Yes | Yes | Through Remotion | Revideo's render API |
| **Version** | 0.2 | 0.8 | 4.x | Months old | 3.17, 0.11 |

All of them render deterministically when scenes follow their rules (time
from the frame number, seeded randomness): the same input gives the same
frames. Each section below has the details and links to the licence and
pricing pages.

## Intentface Launch

A React video engine, a CLI, a local studio and a set of Claude Code skills,
built around one job: a launch video of a software product, made from the
product itself. Claude reads and runs your app, agrees a brief and script
with you, cuts to a music track's beats, sketches a storyboard you comment
on, then builds and renders shot by shot.

**Good for**

- **The real product.** Videos import your components and data. Capture
  scripts drive the running app one exact frame at a time, and DOM and CSS
  snapshots keep close-ups sharp at any zoom.
- **Shaping it to a team.** Core is brand-neutral. A brand add-on carries a
  team's fonts, logos, design, and motion and copy rules. `launch/CLAUDE.md`
  holds how your team works, and rules written there after one video carry
  into the next.
- **Review before building.** You comment per shot on a storyboard and
  animatic before anything heavy is built.
- **Checks a person usually does by eye.** `pnpm launch check` measures every
  line of text on screen for reading time, size, contrast and room from the UI
  beside it, and flags end frames that differ from the approved logo.
- **Craft add-ons.** 3D with live UI on panels and real depth of field, and
  WebGPU shaders on the video's clock.
- **MIT throughout**, with no Remotion dependency and no telemetry.

**Know before you choose it**

- **Young.** On npm since 13 October 2026; 0.1.1 and 0.2.0 shipped broken
  installs, fixed in 0.2.1. APIs, commands and skills will change.
- **Tested on one setup.** An Apple M2 Pro Mac with Claude Opus 5.5 at High
  effort. Other operating systems, GPUs and models are untested, and without a
  GPU rendering is much slower. See [Requirements](/requirements).
- **Claude Code in practice.** The skills install in other agents through the
  skills CLI, but we have not tested that.
- **Built for your source and a running app.** Captures can record any URL,
  but the method expects the product's source and a demo or seeded mode you
  control.
- **Narrow.** Product launch videos, not explainers, captions, decks or general
  motion graphics.
- **No hosted rendering**, and no editor beyond the review studio.
- **No music.** You bring or generate a track; the video is cut to it.

## HyperFrames

[HyperFrames](https://hyperframes.dev), from HeyGen, renders HTML, CSS, media
and seekable animations to deterministic MP4: "Write HTML. Render video.
Built for agents." It is used in production at HeyGen, Apache-2.0
([licence](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/LICENSE)), and
moving fast.

**Good for** a broad range of videos made by an agent: launch videos from a
product URL, script or brief (its `/product-launch-video` skill, which shares
a name with ours), faceless explainers, PR-to-video, captions, talking-head
recuts, motion graphics and music videos. It renders in headless Chrome with
FFmpeg, its 21 skills come as plugins for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini
CLI and GitHub Copilot, it captures a site's visuals and brand from a URL, and it renders
locally or in HeyGen's cloud, on AWS Lambda or on Google Cloud Run. There is
also a Studio app for working without code. The breadth is impressive.

**Know before you choose it:** it is HTML-first, with no React runtime. A
skill ports Remotion projects to HTML and GSAP, one way, so a product's own
React components do not drop in. Cloud renders and some voice and music
features use a HeyGen account. [Docs](https://hyperframes.heygen.com/introduction).

## Remotion

[Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev) is the established React framework for
programmatic video, and a solid choice for production work: Studio, an
embeddable Player, rendering on AWS Lambda, on Vercel or in the browser, a
large ecosystem and [official agent skills](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills).
It has been in production for years, and much of programmatic video, ours
included, builds on ideas it made common.

**Good for** React video of any kind: video inside your own app, templated
videos at scale, and anything that benefits from a mature ecosystem and
documentation. If you know Remotion, our engine will feel familiar
(`useFrame` for `useCurrentFrame`, `Sequence`, `interpolate`, `spring`).

**Know before you choose it:** it is free for individuals, non-profits and
companies of up to 3 employees, and a for-profit company above that needs a
company licence, which funds the project. Remotion 5.0 (announced, not yet
released) changes the terms slightly, for example counting contractors
towards team size. Read the current [licence](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license)
and [pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing).

## snapcn

[snapcn](https://snapcn.dev), by Sri Nath under the community org Shadcn Labs,
is a shadcn-style registry of Remotion components for product demo videos:
AI chat streams, terminal sessions, device frames, captions, logo stings and
whole scenes. You install components with `npx shadcn add`, and they are
copied into your repo as source you own. It is a few months old and very
active. We really dig shadcn/ui, and snapcn's approach is a cool one.

**Good for** polished, recreated product-demo shots in a Remotion project,
themed with shadcn/ui's tokens. It ships skills (including a launch-video
workflow), a Claude Code plugin, an MCP server with Pro, and a browser editor
that exports MP4, so you can try it without installing anything.

**Know before you choose it:** it needs a Remotion project, and Remotion's
licence applies. Free components are MIT; Pro components and the MCP server
are paid, and company work needs its Commercial plan
([pricing](https://snapcn.dev/docs/pricing)). Shots are recreations of a UI
rather than captures of yours, which also means they need no running app.
Captured UI and recreated shots can sit in one film.

## Motion Canvas and Revideo

[Motion Canvas](https://motion-canvas.io) animates on a canvas with
TypeScript generator functions, with a web editor to sync scenes to audio. It
is MIT and popular for explainers; its last stable release was 3.17.2, in
December 2024. [Revideo](https://github.com/midrender/revideo), forked from
it and MIT, adds a headless render API and a React player, and is the engine
behind [Midrender](https://midrender.com).

**Good for** diagrams, technical explainers and videos generated in batches,
where the visuals are drawn rather than taken from a web app.

**Know before you choose it:** scenes are drawn on a canvas, not DOM, so a web
product's components do not drop in. Revideo sends anonymous render telemetry
by default (`DISABLE_TELEMETRY=true` turns it off).

## Manim

[Manim](https://www.manim.community) (the community edition, MIT) animates
maths and algorithms in Python, and is the standard there. It is not aimed at
product videos.

## An agent with no framework

Ask a coding agent for a launch video with nothing installed, and it will
usually write a script that screenshots pages with Playwright or Puppeteer
and pipes them into ffmpeg.

**Good for** a quick one-off or a proof of concept: you own every line and
there is nothing to learn.

**Know before you choose it:** each video rebuilds the same plumbing:
deterministic timing, parallel rendering, audio and beat sync, transitions,
legibility checks, safe zones for vertical cuts. That plumbing is what every
tool on this page provides.

## Generated footage

Video models such as [Veo](https://deepmind.google/models/veo/),
[Runway](https://runway.com) and [Kling](https://kling.ai) make footage from a
prompt, and they can start from images, including screenshots of your UI.
Combining generated footage with launch materials is an avenue we are
exploring. Video models are still expensive and heavy to run, so they are not
part of the harness yet. A clip you generate yourself goes in a video's
`media/`, with its licence recorded, and plays in a shot with `Clip`, beside
the product's real UI. The same works in the code-based tools above.
