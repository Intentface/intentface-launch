There is no one way to ask for a video. Claude follows the same method each
time (discover, script, music, sketch, build, check), and how much you give it
up front only changes how many questions it asks. Start small and add material
as you go, or hand over everything at once. These recipes are starting points:
mix them, change them, and write down the ones that work for your team.

## Start from one sentence

Point Claude at the product and say what the video is for:

> Look at my app in ../scheduler and make a feature release video about our
> scheduling feature, 30 seconds.

Claude reads the code and docs, runs the app, and looks at it. Then it asks the
few things only you know, each with suggested answers from what it found: the
moment a new user says "oh", who will watch and where, the pace, the music.
Anything your sentence already said ("calm", "for LinkedIn", "no music yet") it
does not ask again.

Run the app's dev server in its demo or seeded mode first, or tell Claude how
to start it. Claude records the real UI from it, so the video uses your
product's own type, colours and data.

## Show what to highlight with screenshots

When you know which screens matter, show them. Make the video first (or let
Claude make it), then put screenshots in the video's `reference/` folder:

```text
launch/videos/scheduling/reference/
  01-empty-week.png
  02-drag-to-book.png
  03-conflict-warning.png
```

> The screenshots in reference/ are the three moments I want: the empty week,
> dragging a slot to book it, and the conflict warning. Build the video around
> those, in that order.

Claude reads each one, finds the same state in the running product and records
it there. A recording of the live UI stays sharp when the camera pushes in; a
screenshot does not. So screenshots in `reference/` guide the video and do not
appear in it, unless you ask for that.

If the product cannot run on your machine (a native app, a staging-only
feature), say so. Claude can build shots from your screenshots or a screen
recording instead, at the resolution they were taken, and tells you up front
what that costs.

## Bring your own images and clips

Put files you want in the video in its `media/` folder, and say what each one
is for:

```text
launch/videos/scheduling/media/
  intro-sting.mp4      # our logo animation
  team.jpg             # photo for the end card
  calendar-sync.mov    # screen recording of the mobile app
```

> Open with media/intro-sting.mp4, cut to the app on the first downbeat. Put
> media/team.jpg behind the end card, dimmed. Use calendar-sync.mov for the
> mobile shot, full frame.

Video files play frame exact (`Clip`), so a clip lands on the same frame in the
studio, in stills and in the render. Another video's render can be used the
same way: a logo sting made once opens every later video.

Record where each file comes from, so `pnpm launch check` and
`pnpm launch licences` know it is cleared: Claude writes a `licences.json` in
the video's folder (`{ "media/team.jpg": { "licence": "Our own photo" } }`).
Your own product and brand files only need a line saying they are yours.

## Music and sound

Intentface Launch cuts the video to a track; it does not make music. You have
three ways to get one:

- **Your own track.** A track you bought, licensed or had made goes in the
  video's `audio/` folder. `pnpm launch beats` finds its beats and sections,
  and the shots are timed to them.
- **Generate one from Claude's brief.** Once the script is agreed, Claude
  writes a brief with the exact length, tempo, sections and hits. You generate
  the track (ElevenLabs Music, Suno and the like) and drop it in `audio/`.
- **No music yet.** Claude picks a tempo and times everything in beats, so a
  track can be briefed to the finished cut later.

Sound effects (clicks, ticks, whooshes) come from a small starter library in
the package. `pnpm launch sfx find whoosh` searches it. For effects it lacks,
Claude can generate them with ElevenLabs:

1. Install the ElevenLabs CLI: `npm install -g @elevenlabs/cli`.
2. Put your API key in `launch/.env` (ignored by git):

   ```sh
   ELEVENLABS_API_KEY=sk_...
   ```

3. Ask for the sound pass once the cut is locked:

   > The cut is locked. Add sound effects: a soft click on each booking, a
   > swell under the end card.

Claude never prints the key. Commercial use of generated sounds needs a paid
ElevenLabs plan, and before generating anything for a sonic logo, turn off
ElevenLabs' setting that shares outputs with other users.

Your own sounds work too. Put sounds for one video in its `audio/sfx/`, and
sounds for every video in the launch folder's `sfx/`, then run
`pnpm launch sfx index` to measure them. A voice-over (recorded or generated)
goes in `audio/voice/`, and the music ducks under it.

## Write down how your team works

Claude reads `launch/CLAUDE.md` whenever it works in the launch folder. Write
your team's way of making videos there, above the add-ons list:
`pnpm launch update` keeps it. For example:

```md
## How we make videos

- Every video opens with the `sting` video's render and ends on our end card.
- 30 seconds for a feature, 60 for a launch. 9:16 cut for every feature video.
- Music: the tracks in launch/music/, licensed for all our videos.
- Calm and precise: long holds, no slams.
- Never show customer names; use the demo workspace.
```

Longer recipes can go in their own files, with a line in `CLAUDE.md`
pointing to them (`Weekly updates: follow launch/recipes/weekly-update.md`).
Claude follows what is written, and asks only what it leaves open.

When a video shows that a skill is wrong or missing something, Claude says
which skill and what it learned. Skills are revised in the packages, so a fix
reaches every repo with `pnpm launch update`.

## Ideas

Ways to use it beyond the one-off launch video. Each is a request, and some
are worth writing down as a recipe.

### A weekly product update, scripted once

The same structure every week, so each episode is quick to make and looks like
the last one: the logo sting, three changes shown working in the product, and
an end card with the date.

Build it once:

> Make a weekly-update video: open with the sting video, then three slots of
> about 8 seconds, each showing one change in the product with a three-word
> label, then an end card with the week's date. 30 seconds, our weekly track.
> Make the three changes and the date the parts that change each week.

Claude builds the first episode and shows the storyboard. After that, each
week is a variant of the same video: the same shots, music and look, with that
week's changes and copy.

> This week's update: the changelog is in CHANGELOG.md under 2026-10-12. Show
> recurring events, the new time-zone picker and faster search. Add it as
> variant w42.

Claude records the three changes, fills the slots, and renders
`out/weekly-update-w42.mp4` with `pnpm launch render weekly-update --variant w42`.
Write the structure in `launch/recipes/weekly-update.md` so the format stays
the same whoever asks.

### A video for every feature release

One look for all of them, set in `launch/CLAUDE.md` (length, opening, music,
pace), so each release video only needs the feature:

> New release video for bulk rescheduling. Same format as our feature videos.

### Every shape from one video

A video can declare other shapes (`formats`, such as 9:16 and 1:1). The layout
reflows for each; it is never a crop. Ask for a vertical cut for social posts
and `pnpm launch render <id> --format vertical` renders it.

### Languages and captions

Captions are part of the video: `render` writes `.srt` and `.vtt` beside the
mp4, and they can be burned into the frame for muted feeds. A language version
is a variant with its own copy and voice-over:

> Add a German variant: translate the on-screen copy and the captions.

### A logo sting

> Animate our logo as a 3 second sting, from logo.svg in media/, with a sonic
> logo. We'll open every video with it.

The logo-animation skill derives the motion from how the mark is built and
makes sure the last frame is exactly the approved logo. Other videos then open
with it.

### A demo for a sales call or a talk

Longer and calmer, at reading pace, with captions and no music:

> A 2 minute walkthrough of the scheduling flow for sales calls. No music,
> captions on, slow enough to talk over.
